The Six-Photo FixThe Red-Flag Checklist

Before you upload anything

Keep the photos that make you look great and like yourself.

You do not need to find a perfect AI image. You need the version that is flattering, believable, and comfortable to stand behind when you meet someone in person.

A strong profile is not six flawless images. It is six photos that make someone think: “He looks good. He looks real. I’d like to know more.”

The 5-second reality check

A quick scorecard for when you have too many good options.

Give each candidate one point for every “yes.” It is not a test—just a fast way to see which image will work hardest for your profile.

Ask yourselfOne point when the answer is yes
Does it make me look good at a glance?+1
Does it clearly look like me now?+1
Does it feel like a normal real moment?+1
Does it add something new to my final six?+1
Would I be happy to be recognised from it?+1
4–5: add it to your shortlist. 3: it may just need one small refinement. 0–2: choose a different version and keep moving.

How to use this

Open the image. Take ten seconds. Trust the useful questions.

Run each candidate through this before it earns a place on your profile. You are not looking for reasons to reject everything—you are looking for the version that gives you the best result.

01

Pick one photo job.

Compare only images that are trying to do the same job: Hook, Make it real, Show your life, and so on.

02

Look at it normally.

First judge it at phone-screen size. Then zoom in once for details that could make the image feel off.

03

Choose an action.

Keep the strongest one, refine a close one, or simply choose a different version. No overthinking required.

The five-question filter

Does this photo earn its place?

01

Would she stop on this?

Is your face clear? Do you look relaxed, current, and attractive at a quick glance? If another version has a warmer expression or cleaner framing, use that one.

02

Would she believe it?

Does it still look recognisably like you today? Check the face, hair, skin, body, hands, teeth, eyes, clothes, and background. You are aiming for “great photo of him,” not “who is that?”

Quick reality check: if something looks a little strange when you zoom in, it will probably feel strange to someone else too. Choose another version or refine that one detail.
03

Does it add something new?

A good profile builds as she scrolls. Pick the version that adds a new angle, setting, outfit, expression, or glimpse of your life—not the sixth variation of the same photo.

04

Does it fit your real life?

You do not need an exciting life to make this work. You only need a moment you could comfortably talk about: a local place, normal plan, hobby, meal, dog, book, event, or small detail that is actually yours.

05

Would you happily meet someone who saw it?

This is the final test. If the answer is yes, it is doing its job. The best dating-profile photo is flattering enough to get attention and honest enough to make that attention feel good in real life.

Things worth catching

A few small details can make a good image feel less real.

Identity drift

You look better, but also younger, slimmer, differently styled, or like someone with a different face. Pick the version that still feels unmistakably you.

AI weirdness

Waxy skin, glassy eyes, over-white teeth, strange ears, hands, objects, text, reflections, or background details. If you notice it, do not try to talk yourself past it.

Too polished

Fake bokeh, studio light, a luxury-looking room, a perfect empty street, or an editorial pose can turn a natural photo into an advert. The friendly, ordinary version will work harder.

Too similar

Two solid photos can still weaken the profile if they repeat the same shirt, pose, café, lighting, or smile. Keep the one that gives the profile more range.

Not actually yours

A dog, hobby, sport, venue, or lifestyle detail only works if you would feel comfortable talking about it. You are not trying to invent a better life; you are making your real one easier to see.

Make the call

Every image gets one simple outcome.

Keep it

It looks good, looks like you, and does a useful job in the profile. Add it to your shortlist.

Refine it

It is nearly there. Ask for one targeted change: more realistic skin, correct hair, a natural hand, or less polished lighting.

Choose another

It is not bad—it just is not the strongest version. Move on and keep the process easy.

If one detail is nearly there

Use a rescue line instead of starting again.

Paste one of these in the same chat with the close result. Keep the fix small so you do not lose the good parts of the image.

Face has drifted

Keep the exact scene, pose, clothing and framing. Make my face, hairline, facial hair and age match my reference photos more closely. Do not change anything else.

It looks too polished

Keep the exact composition, pose, clothing and identity. Make this look like an ordinary rear-camera phone photo with natural skin texture, normal lighting and less AI polish.

Hands or background look strange

Keep my identity, clothes, pose and scene unchanged. Correct only the hands and background details so they look natural, proportionate and realistic.

The expression is not quite right

Keep the same identity, outfit and setting. Give me a small, relaxed, natural expression as if a friend has just said something funny. Do not make it look posed.

See the difference

Three simple decisions, made easier.

Keep

Clear face, natural smile, ordinary setting, and it adds a different side of you. It may not be “perfect”—it is simply the strongest profile photo.

Refine

The composition is great and it looks like you, but the skin is a little too smooth. Use the realism rescue line, then compare the new version.

Choose another

The image is flattering, but the hair, face, or setting feels unlike your real life. A more believable version will convert better in the long run.

One last look at the full set

Make sure the six work together.

Clear faces first

Have at least two clear, face-forward photos near the start. Let someone quickly see who they would be meeting.

Give every image a job

Make sure each one adds a new angle, outfit, setting, or real-life detail instead of repeating the same moment.

Keep one easy opener

Include one visible detail that makes replying simple: a hobby, dog, meal, local find, project, activity, or story.

Make it feel like one life

The clothes, face, hair and places do not need to match exactly. They just need to look like the same current person.

You are ready

Pick the photos you are happy to be recognised from.

That is the whole goal. The right set will look like the best version of your real life—not a life you have to explain away.

  1. Choose the strongest image for each photo job.
  2. Use the five questions when you are torn between two versions.
  3. Upload the six that make you feel both confident and comfortable.

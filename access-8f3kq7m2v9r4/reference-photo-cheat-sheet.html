Reference Photo Cheat Sheet

Your outputs are only as good as the photos you give the AI.

You don't need professional photos. You need a set that clearly shows what you actually look like from different angles.

What Makes a Good Reference Photo

A good reference photo:

  • Clearly shows your full face
  • Has decent lighting
  • Isn't heavily filtered or blurry
  • Doesn't have sunglasses hiding your eyes or a hat hiding your hairline
  • Isn't shot from a ridiculous fisheye angle
  • Doesn't have someone else's face right next to yours

The AI is trying to understand your face shape, eyes, nose, jaw, hair, skin tone, build, and proportions. The clearer you show those things, the more realistic your outputs will be.

Use Different Angles

Don't upload 10 versions of the same selfie. Give it a spread:

  • Straight-on
  • Slightly turned left and right
  • A proper side angle
  • Looking slightly up or down
  • Smiling and neutral expressions

You don't need to obsess over exact angles. The point is to stop the AI guessing what the rest of your face looks like.

Include Your Body for Full-Body Photos

If you're making restaurant, walking, travel, outdoor, or full-body dating photos, include body references too:

  • One standing front-on
  • One standing at a slight angle
  • One normal seated shot
  • One wider candid where your proportions are clear

This helps stop the AI randomly making you taller, broader, thinner, or built completely differently.

Use Current Photos

Use photos that reflect how you look now. Mixing five-year-old photos with current ones—especially if your haircut, beard, weight, glasses, or hair colour has changed—makes the AI choose between two versions of you.

Use the version of yourself you actually want showing up on your profile.

Variety Beats Perfection

Your reference photos don't need to be good dating photos. They are training material.

A boring bathroom photo with a clear view of your face can be more useful than a great holiday shot where your face is tiny. We're not putting these photos on Tinder—we're giving the AI information.

What to Avoid

Try not to use:

  • Blurry night-out or group photos
  • Heavy Instagram or Snapchat-style beauty filters
  • Sunglasses or faces hidden by hands
  • Extreme shadows across your face
  • Photos where you're tiny in the frame
  • Ten photos from the same angle
  • Photos where you've obviously changed appearance

One or two imperfect photos won't ruin anything. Just don't build the whole set out of them.

Easy 10-Photo Reference Set

1. Straight-on face photo — neutral expression

2. Straight-on smiling photo — clear teeth and a natural smile

3. Face turned slightly left

4. Face turned slightly right

5. Clear side-profile photo

6. Upper-body photo — standing or seated

7. Full-body front photo

8. Full-body slight-angle photo

9. Natural candid — not staring straight into the camera

10. Another clear, recent photo that fills a gap in the set

That's enough for most tools in this guide to get a decent understanding of what you look like.

If You're Using 15–20 Photos

Don't just add duplicates. Use the extra slots for more information:

  • Face: 8–10 photos — front, both sides, smiling, neutral, and different lighting
  • Upper body: 3–4 photos — sitting, standing, and different angles
  • Full body: 3–5 photos — front, side, slight angle, and walking/candid if available

Quick Rule

If someone looked at your reference set, could they answer these questions?

  • What does his face look like from every angle?
  • What is his current haircut and facial hair?
  • What is his body shape?
  • What does he look like when he smiles?

If yes, you're good to go.
